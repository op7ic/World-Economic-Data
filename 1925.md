# MACRO/MICRO GLOBAL ECONOMIC ASSESSMENT - YEAR: 1925

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

1925 was a year of postwar normalization with a fragile foundation. The leading industrial economies were no longer in the immediate emergency conditions of 1919-1923: German hyperinflation had been stopped, the Dawes Plan had improved the mechanism for reparations and foreign lending, and the United States was in the middle of a strong 1920s expansion. Yet the apparent return to order depended heavily on gold-standard discipline, dollar credit, and politically sensitive wage and price adjustments. The clearest symbol was the United Kingdom's April 1925 return to gold at the prewar parity, a decision that restored an old monetary rule but imposed deflationary pressure on export industries and coal.

The global economy was still asymmetric. The United States was the dominant economy in the available MPD 2023 ranking, with GDP of about 1.30 trillion 2011 international dollars and a population of about 116.3 million. The United Kingdom, India, Germany, the former USSR, France, Japan, and Italy formed the next tier among countries with available GDP data. China should be treated as a first-order missing observation, not a small economy: MPD 2023 records a large 1925 population but lacks the matching GDP-per-capita value. This is the largest single ranking uncertainty for 1925.

The year was economically defined by three interacting forces: restoration of international monetary rules, uneven real recovery, and rapid microeconomic change in transport, consumer durables, electrification, chemicals, and mass distribution. The United States and Canada benefited from automobiles, construction, consumer credit, and securities-market enthusiasm; Canadian real GDP rose 10.93% and U.S. GDP 1.72% in the Maddison Project Database 2023. Germany posted large reconstructed growth (11.24%) from a disrupted base, although a League of Nations industrial index shows German production falling in the second half of the year. The former USSR record shows a large rise, but its territory and source joins are unmatched, so no growth rate is attributed to it. Britain faced the opposite problem: policy credibility improved, but the real economy absorbed the cost through pressure on wages, coal, shipbuilding, and export competitiveness.

Measured by available evidence, 1925 was not yet a crisis year, but it was a year in which several vulnerabilities became more visible. The international system depended on the recycling of US capital to Europe and Latin America. Commodity producers benefited unevenly from recoveries in oil, metals, and agricultural markets; world export volume rose 7.52% in the Federico-Tena reconstruction. Equity markets, especially in New York, were buoyant: the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 30.0%. With hindsight, 1925 sits in the middle of the build-up to the late-1920s credit and asset boom, but that outcome was not predetermined from the information available in 1925.

## 2. KEY THEMES OF 1925

**1. Return to gold and the gold-exchange standard.** The most consequential policy theme was monetary reconstruction. Britain announced the return to the prewar sterling parity in the budget speech of 28 April 1925, and the Gold Standard Act received assent on 13 May. Convertibility was restored for gold bullion rather than coin, reinforcing the view that stable currencies and fixed exchange rates were prerequisites for normal trade and finance. The significance was enormous: sterling was a reserve and invoicing currency, and its return helped legitimize the postwar gold-exchange standard. The forward link was negative as well as stabilizing. The return to gold put pressure on prices and wages in Britain and helped make the interwar monetary system more brittle.

**2. US financial dominance and outward capital flows.** The United States was the largest economy in the available MPD 2023 ranking and had become a central source of international credit. US banks and investors financed European stabilization, Latin American borrowers, utilities, and infrastructure. The Federal Reserve arranged a two-year gold credit of $200 million for the Bank of England, alongside $100 million of private credit for the British government. The significance in economic history is that the old London-centered system was becoming a dollar-sterling system. The forward link was the dependence of European stabilization on continued US capital-market access.

**3. Recovery from war, inflation, and political disruption.** Germany, Austria, and parts of Eastern Europe were still recovering from the war, territorial rearrangements, and the inflationary crises of the early 1920s. Germany's MPD 2023 real GDP growth for 1925 is about 11.2 percent, but that must be read as recovery from abnormal conditions rather than normal trend growth. The former USSR record shows a raw change of about 27.8 percent, consistent with NEP-era rebound and reconstruction from civil war devastation, but because the territory of its national-income source and its population series are not matched, that change is not a comparable growth rate. These numbers are not directly comparable to mature-economy growth rates.

**4. Mass production, consumer durables, and urban services.** The United States led the microeconomic transformation of the year. Automobiles, electrical appliances, radio, chain retail, consumer finance, road building, and suburban real estate changed household budgets and firm organization. Ford was still central to automobile mass production, but 1925 was also the year in which Chrysler emerged as a significant corporate competitor. The forward link was the late-1920s consumer-credit and securities-market expansion.

**5. Commodity recovery without a uniform commodity boom.** Oil, copper, and some metals improved from the postwar slump, but agriculture remained regionally uneven. The United States still faced farm-sector distress despite urban prosperity. Petroleum output was rising, and the Federal Reserve Bulletin reported rising crude prices in early 1925 after reduced output. The League of Nations production indices (1926=100) show cereals rising from 86 to 100 and metals from 86 to 97. The long-run significance was the transition toward oil, automobiles, and electrified industry as central growth mechanisms.

**6. Colonial and semi-colonial economies as major macro actors.** India and Indonesia were among the largest available GDP economies in MPD 2023 because of population size, agriculture, and colonial export systems. Their macro stories cannot be reduced to European policy. India was a giant low-income colonial economy with agriculture, railways, textiles, and fiscal constraints shaped by British rule. Indonesia was a plantation and resource economy integrated into Dutch imperial trade. These economies were deeply affected by commodity prices, exchange regimes, and imperial trade rules.

### 3B. Monetary & Financial Conditions

The year operated inside the reconstructed interwar gold-standard environment. This was not the classical pre-1914 gold standard in a simple sense; it was increasingly a gold-exchange system in which central-bank reserves, sterling, dollars, and official cooperation mattered. The United States Federal Reserve used discount policy, bankers' acceptances, and open-market operations; by the mid-1920s these instruments were understood as mechanisms that influenced money-market rates. The Federal Reserve Bank of New York raised its discount rate from 3% to 3.5% on 27 February and did not raise it again until January 1926. Britain returned to gold in April 1925, and Bank Rate ended the year at 5% after averaging 4.58%. Germany had already stabilized through the Rentenmark and Reichsmark reforms, and the Dawes Plan eased the external transfer problem. The Bank of Japan cut its commercial-bill discount rate to 7.30% from 15 April.

Financial conditions were easier in the United States than in deflationary Britain. The Dow Jones Industrial Average ended 1925 much higher than 1924, reflecting a broad bull market in industrial equities. Federal Reserve reporting distinguished first-half releases of commercial-loan funds from second-half seasonal commercial borrowing and securities lending, and acknowledged securities and localized real-estate speculation while denying a broad commodity inventory boom. Bond-market and yield-curve data are not uniformly available for all major countries in a comparable form. International credit conditions were favorable enough to support foreign lending, but the system was vulnerable because stabilization depended on continued investor confidence.

Central-bank institution building extended beyond Europe. Banco de México began operations on 1 September 1925 with monopoly note-issue powers, though it also undertook commercial banking and public distrust of paper money limited its immediate monetary control. In Chile, Decree Law 486 of 22 August created the central bank under the Kemmerer reform program; it opened to the public in 1926. Argentina had suspended gold convertibility in 1914 and did not resume it until 1927, and had no central bank until 1935.

### 3C. Trade & Geopolitics

No single global trade agreement dominated 1925. Instead, the key issue was the reconstruction of prewar-style trade and payments under new political borders and reparations obligations. World export volume rose 7.52% in the Federico-Tena reconstruction. The Locarno Treaties improved confidence in Western Europe, which mattered for credit and business expectations. Colonial economies remained integrated through imperial preference, shipping networks, and commodity exports. The Soviet Union remained outside normal capitalist capital markets but used trade, concessions, and NEP mechanisms selectively.

### 3D. Commodities & Energy

Energy transition was visible but incomplete. Coal still mattered for Britain, Germany, and heavy industry, but oil was becoming central to transport and industry. The United States was the dominant petroleum producer. Metals demand improved with construction, autos, electrification, and capital-goods recovery. Commodity-producing economies, including Canada, Australia, Argentina, Brazil, Mexico, Chile, and Indonesia, were exposed to terms-of-trade shifts.

### 3E. Labor & Demographics

Labor-market statistics are not comparable across countries in 1925. Definitions of unemployment varied, and many agrarian economies did not measure unemployment in a modern labor-force sense. Broad patterns are clearer than precise rates: urban industrial employment improved in the United States and Germany; Britain had persistent unemployment in coal, shipbuilding, textiles, and export trades (7.76% in the Bank of England's labor-force-consistent reconstruction); colonial economies were shaped by rural underemployment, plantation labor, and migration. U.S. unemployment was 4.0% in Lebergott's reconstruction. Demographically, China, India, and the former USSR were population giants, and urbanization continued in North America, Europe, Japan, and parts of Latin America. World population rose about 1.11% to 1,927.7 million.

**Economic narrative:**

The United States was the core growth and finance engine of the available world economy. Output expanded moderately in 1925 after the 1924 trough, and the NBER chronology places the country in an expansion that ran from mid-1924 to late 1926. The macro story was a combination of productivity growth, construction, consumer durables, road building, electrification, and strong securities markets. Consumer prices rose 2.3% after a flat 1924, and Lebergott's unemployment estimate fell from 5.5% to 4.0%.

The Federal Reserve's annual report distinguishes first-half releases of commercial-loan funds from second-half seasonal borrowing and securities lending, and noted securities and localized real-estate speculation while denying a broad commodity inventory boom.

Financial distress remained geographically concentrated. The Federal Reserve recorded bank suspensions falling from 777 in 1924 to 612 in 1925 (-21.24%), with deposits of suspended banks falling from $213.444 million to $172.900 million (-18.99%); these are closures reported in the year, not all permanent failures.

**Micro/sectoral highlights:** Microeconomically, automobiles were central. General Motors, Ford, and the newly formed Chrysler represented different strategies in mass production, marketing, and product differentiation. Consumer credit and installment buying spread, allowing households to bring forward purchases of cars, appliances, and radios. Banking conditions were generally favorable, but rural banks and farm regions remained weaker than the urban-industrial economy. Total bank loans to the nonfinancial private sector in the Macrohistory Database rose 8.66%, the investment-output ratio was 15.84% (+0.76 pp), and real consumption per person in the Barro-Ursúa reconstruction rose 4.51%. The Macrohistory nominal house-price index rose 6.62%. Newspaper asking-price indices rose 3.64% in New York City and 4.31% in Boston, while asking rents fell 4.23% and 4.11%; these are quality-adjusted asking prices sampled mostly near the end of May, not transaction prices.

**Economic narrative:**

Britain's defining event was the return to gold. The decision was intended to restore confidence and London's financial position, but it also locked sterling to a parity that many contemporaries and later economists regarded as overvalued. The macro tension was between financial credibility and industrial competitiveness. Bank Rate rose from 4% to 5% by year-end, and unemployment rose to 7.76% even as real GDP grew 4.86%.

The return was announced on 28 April and the Gold Standard Act received assent on 13 May. Section 1 ended the obligation to redeem notes in gold coin and required the Bank of England to sell gold bullion in bars of approximately 400 fine troy ounces at £3 17s 10½d per standard ounce. The Federal Reserve arranged a two-year $200 million gold-credit facility, alongside $100 million of private credit for the government; the Federal Reserve's annual report states that its facility had not been drawn, so the authorized support was not an actual $300 million inflow.

**Micro/sectoral highlights:** At the micro level, finance, shipping, and international services retained global importance, while coal, textiles, shipbuilding, and older export industries faced pressure. The decision to return to gold shaped wage bargaining, coal costs, and the conditions that would feed into the 1926 General Strike. Britain looked stable in financial terms, but its industrial base showed structural weakness. Total bank loans in the Macrohistory Database rose 7.44%, the investment-output ratio was 9.32% (+0.73 pp), real consumption per person rose 2.01%, and the Macrohistory nominal house-price index rose 11.30%. The Campbell-Grossman-Turner December share-price index (January 1869=100) rose from 134.0 to 140.8 (+5.07%), with domestic shares +3.36% and blue chips +6.40%; the Macrohistory equity total return was 15.12%.

**Economic narrative:**

Germany's 1925 economy was a recovery economy. The inflation trauma had been stabilized, the Dawes Plan improved reparations arrangements, and foreign capital inflows supported investment and confidence. Growth was strong because the economy was moving away from abnormal disruption, not because all structural problems were solved. Bank loans to the private sector nearly doubled (+90.49%) as credit was rebuilt after stabilization.

The annual rebound conceals a late-year industrial reversal. The League of Nations manufacturing and basic-industry index (1926=100) was 110 in April and May but 96 in December, a 12.73% fall from the spring level; unlike the U.S. and Canadian columns, it is not seasonally adjusted. ### 5B. Corporate landscape

Data for global company rankings by revenue or market capitalization in 1925 are not available in a standardized global table. Qualitatively, the largest and most important firms included US automobile, oil, steel, electrical, and utility companies; British banks, shipping, oil, and imperial trading firms; German chemicals and electrical firms; and Japanese zaibatsu groups. Chrysler Corporation was founded in 1925, making it one of the most important corporate events of the year. IG Farben was formed in 1925 through the consolidation of major German chemical firms, creating a chemical giant with long-run industrial significance; BASF dates the six-company merger to 2 December 1925, so the year contained less than a month of combined operation.

### 5C. Innovation & technology

The key economic technologies were not single laboratory breakthroughs but diffusion mechanisms: electrification, assembly-line manufacturing, radio broadcasting, telephone networks, chemical synthesis, refrigeration, and motor transport. The United States was the leader in consumer durables and mass distribution. Germany remained strong in chemicals and engineering. Japan was building industrial capacity through textiles, heavy industry, and imperial supply chains.

### 5D. Labor markets & human capital

Labor markets were divided between modern wage labor and vast agricultural or informal sectors. In Britain, union power and unemployment in old export industries were central; unemployment rose to 7.76% in the Bank of England reconstruction. In Germany, stabilization and industrial recovery improved employment conditions but did not eliminate political and distributional conflict, and the late-year fall in industrial production weakened employment. In the United States, urban industrial wages and productivity were stronger, but farm households remained under pressure; Lebergott's unemployment estimate fell to 4.0%. In India, cotton-mill employment fell 2.24% while looms and spindles increased. Education and skill formation were becoming more important in engineering, clerical work, and public administration.

### 6C. Currencies

Sterling's return to gold was the currency event of the year: the pound averaged 482.8944 US cents, up 9.32% from 441.7064 in 1924, close to the prewar parity. The dollar remained fixed to gold through the official gold price. Germany's currency had stabilized after the Rentenmark/Reichsmark reforms, averaging 23.8013 US cents per Reichsmark. Many countries were either returning to gold, preparing stabilization, or managing currencies in relation to sterling, dollars, or gold. On Federal Reserve annual-average quotations, the Argentine gold peso rose 16.96%, the rupee 14.10%, the milreis 11.48%, the peseta 7.55% and the florin 5.10% against the dollar, while the French franc fell 8.97% and the lira 8.73%; the yen was nearly unchanged (-0.36%). These are annual means of noon New York cable-buying rates, not year-end quotations.

## 7. RISKS, CRISES & BLACK SWANS

Active risks in 1925 included Britain's overvalued sterling parity, Germany's dependence on foreign lending, unresolved reparations politics, commodity-export volatility, fragile banking systems in several countries, and weak agricultural incomes in parts of North America and Europe. These were not all visible as imminent crisis triggers in 1925, but they were visible as structural vulnerabilities. U.S. bank suspensions, though lower than in 1924, still numbered 612, and in Japan postponed bad-loan disposal left balance sheets fragile.

Near-misses and latent risks included a reversal of US capital flows, a downturn in commodity prices, labor conflict in Britain's coal industry, and the late-year downturn in German industrial production. Hindsight risk: the 1925-1928 boom in US equities and credit would later look like part of the pre-1929 build-up. That conclusion should be labeled hindsight, because in 1925 contemporaries could plausibly interpret rising output, stable prices, and securities-market gains as evidence of successful stabilization.

## 8. YEAR-OVER-YEAR DELTA (1925 vs. 1924)

The largest structural delta was monetary: Britain returned to gold in 1925, making the gold-exchange restoration more credible but also more deflationary for British industry; Bank Rate rose from 4% to 5% and sterling's annual average rose 9.32% against the dollar. Germany moved from post-stabilization normalization into rapid recovery, with growth of 11.24% after 17.06%, though production weakened late in the year. The former USSR record continued to rise, but no comparable growth rate can be attributed to it. The United States moved further into expansion after the 1924 trough, with consumer prices up 2.3% after a flat year and unemployment down from 5.5% to 4.0%. Canada rebounded strongly in the available data (+10.93% after +1.51%), and Czechoslovakia posted high growth. France slowed sharply, from 12.51% to 0.44%, and Argentina moved from 7.76% growth to a slight contraction. World export volume rose 7.52%.

Risks that eased: German hyperinflation was no longer the central European emergency; US recession conditions from 1923-1924 had passed; foreign lending conditions were more favorable; U.S. bank suspensions fell by about a fifth. Risks that emerged or intensified: Britain's industrial competitiveness problem became sharper; dependence on US capital exports deepened; equity-market optimism grew; inflation accelerated in Italy and the franc and lira weakened; and several commodity exporters remained vulnerable despite improved demand.

## 9. DATA CONFIDENCE & GAPS

High-confidence relative data: United States, United Kingdom, Germany, France, Netherlands, Belgium, Sweden, Canada, and Australia have stronger statistical traditions and more accessible historical accounts than most economies of the period. Medium-confidence data: Italy, Spain, Japan, Czechoslovakia, Austria, Argentina, Brazil, Mexico, and Indonesia. Low-confidence or high-uncertainty data: Former USSR and other non-market or post-imperial reconstructed series. Even in statistically rich economies, unemployment, fiscal, territorial and property measures are reconstructions with specific limits.

The largest GDP ranking gap is China. MPD 2023 full data gives China population in 1925 but no GDP-per-capita value for that year, so no 1925 China GDP is assigned by interpolation. Because China was demographically huge and appears as a major economy in adjacent historical benchmarks, its missing value may materially alter ranks 2-5. The 1924 ranking included an interpolated China at rank 2, so rank numbers in the two years are not directly comparable; among economies with annual observations the top 20 is unchanged. The ranking records are source-defined territories: the 54 records with 1925 observations are not a disjoint world partition, partial frontier overlaps are unresolved, and the summed 4.39 trillion is not world GDP. Former USSR growth is not shown because its national-income source, which distinguishes imperial and interwar territories and calendar and economic years, has not been matched to the population series; the raw +27.81% change is given in Notes only. India is undivided India dated to a 1 October convention, and Germany uses 1936 frontiers.

Several indicators changed basis from 1924 or rest on conflicting sources. Exchange rates are shown as Federal Reserve annual means of noon New York cable-buying rates in US cents per unit, where the 1924 values were Macrohistory annual levels in local currency per USD; the year-over-year changes are computed within the Federal Reserve series, and the United Kingdom row also gives the Macrohistory level. The Mexico rate is also on this basis in 1925, unlike the pesos-per-USD reconstruction used in some earlier years. United Kingdom unemployment is shown from the Bank of England millennium dataset (7.76%) because the Macrohistory series has no 1925 value, whereas the 1924 figure was the Macrohistory value (7.2%, against 7.12% in the Bank of England dataset). United Kingdom debt is 176.97% of GDP in the Macrohistory Database but 173.26% in the Bank of England dataset, the current account 0.94% against 0.93%, and the budget balance +0.72% (fiscal-year central government) against -1.09% (calendar-year public-sector net lending). The Federal Reserve's 1925 annual report gives 777 U.S. bank suspensions in 1924, while an earlier count for 1924 gives 759; the later figure is used for the comparison. The Bank of Japan discount rate of 7.30% is the Head Office commercial-bill rate in force from 15 April. Germany's CPI change for 1924 is not shown because that index spans two currencies, and the large one-year fall in Italy's debt ratio (-40.24 points) should be read with caution.

Policy rates are shown only where an official central-bank rate with a documented date exists (United States, United Kingdom, Japan); market, private-discount or deposit rates for Germany, France, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands, Belgium, Australia, Sweden and India appear in Notes only. Budget balances are Data not available throughout because fiscal-year revenue and expenditure are not matched to calendar-year GDP; the arithmetic ratios are in Notes. The Belgian current-account field is a merchandise trade balance and is not shown as a current account. Macrohistory Database ratios use its own nominal GDP, never the MPD PPP level; its German current-account denominator is linked through Ritschl-Spoerer national-product growth rather than an observed level. House-price series are heterogeneous: Canada's measures replacement value per dwelling, the Netherlands' rests on one Amsterdam street, and the U.S. city indices are asking prices.
